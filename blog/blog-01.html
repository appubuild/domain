How to Choose a Domain Name: 10 Practical Tests Before You Register

October 20, 2026DomainCraft Editorial8 minute read
Domain namingBrand strategyChecklist

Choosing a domain can feel like a tiny decision with a surprisingly long shadow. The name appears in emails, search results, packaging, presentations, and conversations. Instead of picking the first available phrase or endlessly generating combinations, put each candidate through the same practical tests. The process below helps you compare names clearly; it does not replace an availability or legal search.

Start with the job the name needs to do

Write one sentence about the project, the people it serves, and the benefit it offers. This is not necessarily a tagline. It is a filter. A name for a neighborhood repair service may need to sound dependable and local, while a creative software product may want a more distinctive, flexible identity. Decide what qualities matter before scoring options, or you may overvalue whichever name sounds clever in the moment.

The ten tests

  1. Say it aloud. Read the candidate in a normal sentence, such as “Visit ___ for details.” Notice whether the sounds run together or feel uncomfortable.
  2. Try the radio test. Ask someone to write the name after hearing it once. If they need repeated spelling instructions, consider a simpler alternative.
  3. Check the spelling. Avoid accidental double letters, unclear word boundaries, and substitutions that people are likely to forget. A name can be distinctive without being difficult.
  4. Look at the whole string. Read the domain with its extension and without capitalization. Check for unintended words or awkward letter combinations that are easy to miss in a logo mock-up.
  5. Check the meaning in context. Search the phrase, initials, and likely pronunciations. Consider slang, translations, and meanings in the languages used by your customers.
  6. Ask whether it can grow. A narrowly descriptive name may become limiting if the business expands into new products, locations, or audiences. Decide whether that specificity is useful or a constraint.
  7. Test the audience fit. Share a short list with a few people who resemble your intended customers. Ask what they expect the product to do and what feeling each name creates. Ask open questions rather than asking which one they “like.”
  8. Search for existing uses. Look for businesses, apps, products, social profiles, and publications using the same or a confusingly similar name. Search results are a first screen, not legal clearance.
  9. Verify the exact domain. Check the precise spelling and extension with a registrar. Confirm the registration price, renewal price, transfer rules, and whether the name is being offered at a premium.
  10. Check legal risk before investing. Search relevant trademark databases in the countries and categories where you will operate. If the name is commercially important or close to an existing mark, speak with a qualified trademark professional.

Use a simple scorecard

Put the top five candidates in a table and rate each on clarity, memorability, audience fit, flexibility, and distinctiveness. Use a one-to-five scale, but add a note explaining every score. The notes matter more than a total: one low score for pronunciation may be a deal-breaker for a podcast, while a local service may prioritize location in the name. Include status columns for domain availability, social handles, and trademark research so an unchecked item cannot be mistaken for a confirmed result.

Good naming is not a hunt for the cleverest word. It is a decision about what your audience can understand, remember, and trust.

What to do after you shortlist

Give the list a day, revisit it, and test the strongest options in real sentences: “I bought it from…”, “Email…”, or “Search for…”. Check more than one extension if it serves your strategy, but do not register confusing alternatives just to collect them. Once you select a candidate, verify ownership, renewal terms, trademark issues, and social identity before announcing the brand. Keep a record of the checks and the date you completed them.
