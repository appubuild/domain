# Survey engine — Phase 3, partial

The survey route lazy-loads MapLibre GL JS and Terra Draw with the MapLibre adapter. The default background is an offline longitude/latitude graticule, not a land dataset. Phase 5 adds an opt-in experimental online OpenStreetMap layer; see [map catalog](map-catalog.md) for attribution, privacy, provider policy and limitations. Neither layer is authoritative and neither may be used to identify legal boundaries.

The editor configures point, line, polygon, rectangle and selection modes, including coordinate drag, midpoint and deletion flags where supported by Terra Draw. Its mode and session history are delegated to Terra Draw. Incomplete drawing guidance features are excluded from the measurement panel. The working session is in memory: navigating away or reloading loses unsaved edits, with a router navigation guard for completed and in-progress features and a beforeunload warning. Explicit cancellation and tool changes require confirmation for in-progress geometry. Headless Chromium tests exercise navigation guards and completed-polygon editing; browser unload prompts are ultimately controlled by the browser. Phase 4 adds browser-local immutable project copies, GeoJSON export and bounded backup/restore; detailed plot metadata remains outstanding. A bounded GeoJSON FeatureCollection import creates new local copies, with the limitations in local-projects.md. Headless Chromium covers save/reopen, including an edited polygon.

The independent `packages/survey-engine` accepts GeoJSON point, line and single-ring polygon geometries in WGS84 longitude/latitude. It rejects invalid coordinates, duplicate polygon vertices, incomplete/zero-area polygons, self-intersections, polygon holes and antimeridian crossings. Turf uses spherical approximations for area and distance, not an ellipsoidal cadastral calculation. Calculations are estimates and should not be relied upon as legal or professional survey results. GeographicLib comparison, high-latitude and antimeridian support, coordinate transformations, precision/tolerance policy and independent reference verification remain necessary. Standard unit conversions use square metres internally; a decimal is 1/100 of the international acre. Local katha/bigha definitions are intentionally not assumed.

Automated tests cover a one-degree equatorial reference, short and high-latitude lines, edge lengths, snapshot classification, invalid geometries and conversion constants. Headless Chromium verifies polygon creation, vertex drag, undo/redo, edited-geometry save/reopen and simulated touch point creation/selection in portrait and creation in landscape. It also exercises line creation, midpoint insertion, right-click vertex deletion, undo/redo, save/reopen and exact exported coordinates, plus rectangle creation, save/reopen and closed-ring export. A simulated portrait touch test completes a three-vertex line by tapping its last point again, inserts a midpoint and saves and reopens the copy; its saved GeoJSON retains the four coordinates. A separate Chromium regression checks that opening a copy after choosing the point tool actually switches the drawing engine to select, not just the toolbar. Polygon/rectangle vertex insertion and deletion, touch vertex removal, more complex touch editing, physical devices, map gesture conflicts and error recovery remain unverified before drawing can be considered complete. The tested vertex deletion interaction requires a right click; no touch alternative is provided. Keyboard-only geometry creation and autosave are not implemented. Edge lengths use the same spherical distance approximation as the perimeter. Cancellation and router navigation have a component test using mocked map and drawing adapters; router history protection and cancellation are also exercised in headless Chromium. MapLibre is a large lazily-loaded chunk; assess actual performance on supported devices.
